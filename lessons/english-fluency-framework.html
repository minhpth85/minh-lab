English Fluency Framework

Speak English fluently with 3 layers

Native speakers don't know more words than you. They were taught a structure for answering: give three details, add an opinion with reasons, then tell a true story.

See the 3 layers Watch the video

Source: YouTube video by Tiffani, an English teacher. Based on an auto-generated transcript and summarized in our own words.

Why you freeze

You answer in one breath, then go silent

Someone asks, "How was your weekend?" and you reply, "It was good." The answer ends, and the conversation dies with it. The problem is not your vocabulary. It is how you structure the answer.

Vấn đề không nằm ở từ vựng mà ở cách tổ chức câu trả lời.

3 layersDetails → Opinion → Story
3 pointsEach layer is built on groups of three
5 WsWho, what, when, where, why: anchors for your story
1
Layer 1 · Details

Give three details, not one

Native speakers rarely stop at one sentence. On a topic, they instinctively add three details. Three is enough to sound full, but short enough that you never ramble.

Người bản xứ thường đưa ra ba chi tiết: đủ đầy nhưng không lan man.

Topic → Detail 1 → Detail 2 → Detail 3
Question: How was your weekend?

Weekend

Too short"It was good."
Layer 1"I had dinner with my old roommates downtown. On Sunday, I tidied up the whole flat. And I slept in until almost noon."
Topic: a trip

Road trip to the coast

Three details"We took a road trip to the coast for three days. We slept in a beach house with a tiny kitchen. The evenings were windy, but the sunsets were stunning."

Each sentence answers one question: where, where we stayed, and what the weather was like.

Topic: a restaurant

A bistro near my office

Three details"There's a little bistro two streets from my office. Their bread is baked in-house every morning. It costs a bit more than fast food, but the staff are very friendly."
Topic: a new hobby

Swimming

Three details"I started swimming laps this spring. I go to the pool three times a week. And I can already swim twenty lengths without stopping."
Also works for common questions: the speaker uses this same structure for "What's your hometown like?" (location, climate, food), "What do you do for work?" (main role, daily tasks, workplace), and for describing your morning, afternoon, and evening.
2
Layer 2 · Opinion + 3 reasons

Share an opinion, then give three reasons

Layer 1 makes you understood. Layer 2 makes you interesting. Instead of only describing things, you take a position and back it up, the same structure used in school essays.

Lớp 1 giúp người nghe hiểu bạn; lớp 2 giúp họ thấy được quan điểm và cá tính của bạn.

Opinion (I think / Honestly…) → Reason 1 → Reason 2 → Reason 3
Trip

Opinion: the trip was exactly what I needed

"Honestly, that trip was exactly what I needed. The place was peaceful, so my mind could slow down. The fresh air helped me sleep better. And the cozy evenings were my favorite part."
Work

Opinion: I like my job

"Honestly, I like my work a lot. It challenges my imagination every week. My colleagues help me when I'm stuck. And I pick up new skills all the time."
Morning

Opinion: I'm a morning person

"Honestly, I'm a fan of the early hours. Things are calm before the rest of the world wakes up. I think more clearly before noon. And starting early gives my whole day a head start."
Afternoon

A less positive opinion

Your opinion doesn't have to be positive. The structure stays the same.

"Honestly, afternoons are my hardest time. My energy drops after lunch. My calendar is usually full of calls. And by four o'clock the day feels endless."

Tip

An opinion can be neutral or negative, as long as you give a clear reason. The key is saying "what I think and why," not just describing what happened.

Mẹo: quan điểm có thể trung lập hoặc tiêu cực, miễn là có lý do rõ ràng.

3
Layer 3 · Personal story + 5 Ws

Tell a true story

This is the layer that makes listeners stop evaluating your English and start listening to you. Most learners tell a story in one flat sentence. A real story has shape: a setting, people, a moment where something goes wrong or shifts, and how it ended.

Một câu chuyện thật có bối cảnh, nhân vật, một bước ngoặt và kết thúc.

1

Setting

Where and when it happened, and who was there.

2

Turn

What went wrong or surprised you.

3

Ending & lesson

How it turned out and what you learned.

Speaker's example: a winter cabin trip

The broken heater

The speaker and her brother rented a mountain cabin one winter. The heater broke on the first night, so they wrapped up in blankets and laughed until the early hours. That glitch became the best memory of the trip, and it reminded her that unplanned moments are the ones we keep.

Bối cảnh (cabin mùa đông) → Biến cố (máy sưởi hỏng) → Kết thúc (cả hai cười đến khuya và đó là kỷ niệm đẹp nhất).

Try writing your own story with the same three parts.

Add the 5 Ws so nothing is missing

After the story, the speaker checks five anchors so the listener has every key detail. The story carries the emotion. The 5 Ws make sure nothing important is missing.

WhoWho was involved? (my brother)
WhatWhat happened? (the heater broke)
WhenWhen? (last winter)
WhereWhere? (a cabin in the mountains)
WhyWhy did it matter? (unplanned moments stay with us)
Restaurant

An unplanned dinner

A friend dragged the speaker to a tiny Italian place she had almost skipped because she was tired. The owner recommended a dish she had never heard of, and it became the best meal she'd had in a year. Lesson: say yes more often.

Question: What do you do for work?

A job that started with a favor

She fell into her career by accident. A friend passed along her resume when she was unemployed. She started in the lowest position, then led one small project that went well, and everything changed. Her answer to "what do you do?" became a story about one opportunity.

Put the 3 layers together

One answer about your morning

The speaker shows how the layers stack. Here is a similar answer in our own words, so you can try it.

Layer 1 · Details

"My alarm goes off around seven. Coffee comes first. After that, I skim the headlines while the house is calm."

Layer 2 · Opinion

"Honestly, I love mornings. I'm an early bird, and I feel more focused before lunch."

Layer 3 · Story

"Yesterday, I had my coffee on the balcony while the sky turned pink. Nobody was calling me, and I realized I almost never allow myself that kind of stillness."

Why it works: Layer 1 helps listeners understand, Layer 2 shows what you think, and Layer 3 makes them remember. Together they form a natural answer without needing any new vocabulary.

Ba lớp kết hợp lại tạo thành một câu trả lời tự nhiên mà không cần thêm từ vựng mới.

Bonus lesson

Thank the people who help you

Near the end, the speaker tells a story about a friend who brought soup and juice when she was sick, without being asked. Her advice: let the people who helped you know how much you appreciate them. It is also a good story to practice Layer 3.

Hãy cho những người đã giúp bạn biết bạn trân trọng họ. Đây cũng là câu chuyện tốt để luyện lớp 3.

15-minute practice

Write in 3 layers every day

The speaker recommends a daily journal written in the three layers. You can start today with your morning.

Gợi ý: viết nhật ký mỗi ngày theo ba lớp, bắt đầu từ buổi sáng của bạn.

Pick a topic

For example: yesterday morning, a recent meal, or something new you did.

Write 3 details (Layer 1)

One short sentence per detail. Read them aloud once.

Add 1 opinion and 3 reasons (Layer 2)

Start with "I think" or "Honestly", then give reasons with "first", "second", "third".

Tell one story with the 5 Ws (Layer 3)

Cover the setting, the turn, the ending, and the lesson. Check who, what, when, where, and why.

Record and listen back

Record a 60-second answer, listen, and mark where you got stuck. That shows which layer needs more practice.
